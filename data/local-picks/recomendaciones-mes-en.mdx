# Activities of the Month in CDMX — November 2026

November in Mexico City brings Day of the Dead, the Revolution anniversary and perfect weather for walking. Here are 8 activities with date, location and price, recommended by locals.

<Reveal once={false} delay={0.1} className="mb-8">
## 1. Day of the Dead Parade
**Date:** November 1, 2026  
**Location:** Paseo de la Reforma, from the Ángel de la Independencia to the Zócalo (Historic Center)  
**Time:** 2:00 PM – 6:00 PM  
**Price:** Free

The most anticipated event of the year. Floats, giant catrinas, music and thousands of people in costume parade down the city's most iconic avenue. Arrive early to get a good spot along the barricades.

**What to eat nearby:** A cup of esquites at any stand at the Zócalo or an oaxacan tamale at the San Juan market.

**Why go:** It's the most photogenic event of the year and captures the essence of Day of the Dead in CDMX.

> **Tip:** Bring water, sunscreen and comfortable shoes. The closest Metro stations are Bellas Artes and Hidalgo.

</Reveal>

<Reveal once={false} delay={0.2} className="mb-8">
## 2. Night of Museums — Day of the Dead
**Date:** November 2, 2026  
**Location:** Several museums in the Historic Center (Palacio de Bellas Artes, National Museum of Art, Templo Mayor Museum)  
**Time:** 6:00 PM – 11:00 PM  
**Price:** Free

Museums open their doors until late with altars, offerings and special activities for Day of the Dead. It's the perfect opportunity to visit those museums you always postpone.

**What to eat nearby:** A café de olla and pan de muerto at Café de Tacuba (Calle de Tacuba 28, Historic Center).

**Why go:** Free culture with a special atmosphere that only happens in November.

</Reveal>

<Reveal once={false} delay={0.3} className="mb-8">
## 3. Civic Military Parade — November 20
**Date:** November 20, 2026  
**Location:** Zócalo and Paseo de la Reforma (Historic Center)  
**Time:** 9:00 AM – 1:00 PM  
**Price:** Free

Civic parade commemorating the anniversary of the Mexican Revolution. Military bands, war bands and historic vehicles parade down the city's main avenue.

**What to eat nearby:** A pambazo at the stands around the Zócalo or some quesadillas at the San Juan market.

**Why go:** It's one of the few civic parades that still maintains its grandeur and massive turnout.

</Reveal>

<Reveal once={false} delay={0.4} className="mb-8">
## 4. National Anthropology Museum — Family Saturday
**Date:** Saturdays in November (7, 14, 21, 28)  
**Location:** National Anthropology Museum (Av. Paseo de la Reforma and Calzada Gandhi, Chapultepec)  
**Time:** 9:00 AM – 7:00 PM  
**Price:** $95 MXN (Sundays free for nationals)

The best museum in the country with the most important pre-Hispanic collection. Saturdays feature guided activities for families with children.

**What to eat nearby:** A corn tamale or atole at the stands outside the museum, in the first section of Chapultepec.

**Why go:** It's essential to understand Mexico, and children enjoy the interactive guided tours.

</Reveal>

<Reveal once={false} delay={0.5} className="mb-8">
## 5. Walk through Coyoacán Market and the Blue House
**Date:** Any day in November  
**Location:** Coyoacán Market and Blue House (Londres 247, Coyoacán)  
**Time:** Market 9:00 AM – 6:00 PM | Blue House 9:00 AM – 5:30 PM  
**Price:** Market free | Blue House $250 MXN (Tuesdays free for seniors and people with disabilities)

Walking through the cobblestone streets of Coyoacán, visiting the crafts market and entering Frida Kahlo's Blue House is a classic that never fails. November has cool weather ideal for walking.

**What to eat nearby:** A cochinita pibil tostada at the market or a café de olla at Café El Jarocho (Av. México 92, Coyoacán).

**Why go:** It's the most bohemian neighborhood in the city and the Blue House is a must-visit to understand Mexican art.

</Reveal>

<Reveal once={false} delay={0.6} className="mb-8">
## 6. Mezcal Tasting in Roma Norte
**Date:** Thursdays and Fridays in November  
**Location:** Mezcalerías in Roma Norte (Álvaro Obregón and surroundings)  
**Time:** 6:00 PM – 12:00 AM  
**Price:** $$ (tastings from $150 MXN)

Roma Norte has the best concentration of mezcalerías in the city. Go with friends to try different mezcals from Oaxaca, Guerrero and Durango in a relaxed atmosphere.

**What to eat nearby:** Suadero tacos at Taquería Orinoco (Álvaro Obregón 156, Roma Norte) or a huitlacoche quesadilla.

**Why go:** It's the perfect plan for a Thursday or Friday night in one of the trendiest neighborhoods in CDMX.

</Reveal>

<Reveal once={false} delay={0.7} className="mb-8">
## 7. Free Concert at the Zócalo
**Date:** Sundays in November (variable, check calendar)  
**Location:** Zócalo of Mexico City (Historic Center)  
**Time:** 12:00 PM – 4:00 PM  
**Price:** Free

The city government usually programs free concerts on Sundays at the Zócalo. From traditional Mexican music to contemporary groups.

**What to eat nearby:** Huitlacoche and squash blossom quesadillas at the stands around the plaza.

**Why go:** Live music outdoors in the heart of the city, ideal for a Sunday with family.

</Reveal>

<Reveal once={false} delay={0.8} className="mb-8">
## 8. Boat Ride in Xochimilco
**Date:** November weekends  
**Location:** Xochimilco Piers (Cuemanco Pier, Xochimilco)  
**Time:** 9:00 AM – 6:00 PM  
**Price:** $$$ (trajinera from $500 MXN per hour, split among passengers)

Sailing in trajineras through Xochimilco's canals is a unique experience. Bring your own food and music, or hire the mariachis who navigate between the boats.

**What to eat nearby:** Fish at the trajineras (grilled fish) or bring a picnic with quesadillas and sodas.

**Why go:** It's the most fun plan for a weekend with friends or family. World Heritage Site and guaranteed fun.

</Reveal>

---

> **General tip:** Check schedules and prices before heading out, as they can change without notice. Many museums offer free admission on Sundays for nationals.
