# September 16: the day the city gets on its feet

September 16 is not just another public holiday. It is the day Mexico City
stands up: the Zócalo fills from the early morning and, for a few hours, the
whole country seems to be in the same square. And yes: there is a Grito, a
chorus, a flag and a lot of people hugging.

## Where

- **The balcony of the National Palace.** From there the Grito is led, in
  Plaza de la Constitución. You get let in at a particular entrance, and the
  square fills long before eleven.
- **The Zócalo floor.** The square becomes the living room of the night of
  the 15th: flags, families and children asleep on a tarp.
- **Paseo de la Reforma.** The military parade on the morning of the 16th.
- **Campo Marte.** The military parade starts and ends there, up close.
- **The Ángel de la Independencia**, in the middle of Reforma.
- **Moneda and Allende.** Where the 15th becomes a neighbourhood party, not
  only protocol.

## What to do

1. **The Grito**, on the night of the 15th, at eleven. The band plays the
   anthem and the crowd shouts "¡Viva México!" along with whoever is
   presiding. Get there early.
2. **The parade on the 16th** in the morning: the standards, the units and the
   schoolchildren in their first-grade uniform.
3. **The flag-raising ceremony**: the square before dawn, with the band
   playing and everyone else quiet.
4. **The pavilions and the stalls** where the street invents a place to eat.
5. **The Zócalo by day**, when the square changes and the children fall
   asleep on the tarp.
6. **The night of the 15th** anywhere else: the party moves to the terraces
   and the parks.

## What to eat

- **Pozole**, with its lettuce, onion and lime. The dish of the date, eaten
  early, before the parade.
- **Tlacoyos**, blue corn with beans, the real ones from the south.
- **Tamales**, or the sweet ones, which in the south are another thing.
- **Esquites and elotes**, grilled on the street, with lime, cheese and
  chile.
- **Aguas frescas and horchata**, and a **concha** or a **maracaná** for the
  sweet.
- **Atole de cajeta** for whoever gets up at four to watch the flag.

## Understanding Mexicans

The Grito is not a speech: it is an **act**. It has been performed in the same
place and the same way since the nineteenth century, and the crowd knows the
moment it comes. That is why the whole square shouts on the same second: it
is not a spectacle you watch, it is one you step into.

And the 16th is a family day. Whoever stayed in the city goes back to their
town or their grandparents' house, and the meal is the one that has been made
for decades. The parade is the public part; the meal is the private one. And
they are the same holiday.
